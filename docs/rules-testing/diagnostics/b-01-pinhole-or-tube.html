Is that a pinhole, or the tube underneath?

Four views of the same part in four situations, so the spatial arrangement is checkable rather than asserted. The question was whether B-01 misses real violations. It does not — the exemplar was broken, not the rule.

Recorded here because the way the rule tells a pinhole from the tube underneath is genuinely non-obvious, and because getting the exemplar geometry right took a specific method that the remaining exemplars need too.

What the shadow data actually says

Part 3700 has seven connection points. Three of them are female cylinders of radius 6 — indistinguishable by size:

[0, 10, 0] axis [0, 0, 1] — the pinhole, running horizontally through the brick
[−10, 24, 0] axis [0, −1, 0] — the underside tube, vertical
[10, 24, 0] axis [0, −1, 0] — the other underside tube, vertical

Radius alone cannot separate them. What does is the caps attribute the shadow library records: the through-hole primitive is caps=none, an anti-stud tube is caps=one. B-01 already tests this, which is why the real build below is correctly left alone.

Where this leaves the rule

The missed detection was my fixture, not the rule. The plate sat 14 LDU adrift in Z, so no edge ever formed and B-01 correctly said nothing. Corrected to land on the hotspot, it fires.

No rule change was warranted. I first reported that B-01 false-positives on underside tubes. That was wrong: it came from a throwaway scan of mine that matched on radius and part class only, skipping the rule's own caps filter. Run properly, B-01 does not fire on this set, nor on any model in a 30-model corpus sample. The discrimination already works.

The method that fixed it generalises. Read the target part's hotspots, then place the mating part so its hotspot coincides in both position and axis. That is how the rest of the exemplars should be built and checked.
